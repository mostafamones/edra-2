# Edra — academic years, levels, tracks and programs

Approved by the owner, 2026-10-10. Calendar and detailed cross-domain workflows remain for their separate reviews.

## Academic structure

An academy chooses the levels it teaches and may define tracks within those levels. These offerings organize student placement, groups and teaching audiences.

Academic years organize the academy’s activity over time. A student’s relationship with the academy continues across years, while their participation, groups and academic work are recorded for the relevant year.

## Levels

A level is a school grade from Edra’s supported catalog. Academies select the levels they offer; they do not create or rename the catalog’s levels.

The release catalog follows the Egyptian school system:

- Preparatory stage: first, second and third preparatory.
- Secondary stage: first, second and third secondary.

The current Grade 9–12 catalog is a development placeholder and will be replaced before the MVP is ready for release. Level names should be consistent across applications.

## Tracks

A track is an academy-defined specialization within one of its offered levels, such as Scientific or Literature. Tracks belong to the academy and level in which they are created.

A level may have no tracks. In that case, students and staff select the level without an additional track choice. Tracks provide more specific placement and teaching audiences within a level.

Track names must be unique within their academy level. Different academies may use different tracks or names for the same level.

## Programs

A program names a student’s level, or their level and track together. For example, Secondary 2 Scientific and Secondary 2 Literature are different programs within the same level.

Every student’s annual participation has a program: a level, with a track when applicable. “Program” is a naming convention for that placement, not a separate academic entity or additional set of rules.

A group and the students placed in it must use compatible academy, academic-year, level and track relationships.

## Maintaining offerings

Owners, Head Admins and Admins manage offered levels and tracks. Instructors can view them and use them within their permitted academic workflows.

Staff may add offerings and remove tracks during an academic year. Removing a track changes its current placement and teaching audiences while preserving historical references and snapshots.

Adding a track to a level does not silently move existing students or groups into it. Historical records remain available when offerings change.

## Removing a track

Owners, Head Admins and Admins may remove a track during the year. Its affected records change as follows:

- Students lose their placement in the removed track and retain their level.
- Groups assigned to the track become level-wide.
- Recurring schedules targeting the track become level-wide.
- One-off schedules retain their original track targeting as a historical name, without depending on the removed track.
- Active assignments targeting the track become level-wide.
- Closed assignments retain their original identity.
- Content units targeting the track become level-wide.

Historical references and snapshots preserve the track identity applicable when the work occurred. Removing a track does not rewrite past attendance, marks or results.

For example, removing both Scientific and Literature from Secondary 2 leaves Secondary 2 as the level-only program. Their groups, recurring schedules, active assignments and content units become level-wide; students retain their level without a track placement.

## Academic years

An academy has at most one active academic year. The active year is the period in which staff organize current groups, student participation, sessions and academic work.

Owners, Head Admins and Admins manage academic years. Instructors use the active year for academic work and can consult earlier years within their ordinary reading permissions.

Opening another active year requires closing the existing one. Preparing a separate upcoming year while the current year remains active is outside the MVP.

## Closing a year

Closing a year is an explicit academy-management action. Reaching the end of its calendar dates does not automatically close it.

Before closure, staff resolve outstanding live sessions, attendance and results. Closure makes the year’s academic records read-only and ends student learning access derived from that year. Authorized staff can continue to consult its history.

The student’s academy profile and Edra account remain intact. Closing a year does not delete either identity or their earlier academic records.

Routine reopening and editing of closed years are outside the MVP. Exceptional correction or repair requires a separately reviewed procedure.

## Continuing into another year

The academy establishes the new year’s groups and student participation explicitly. Previous students are not automatically enrolled, advanced to another level or given new learning access merely because a year opens.

Returning students retain their academy identity and history. Their new annual participation records the level, track and group applicable to the new year.

Reusable learning material remains part of the academy’s library. Its availability in a new year follows the content-release and access rules for that year.

## Related rules

Student enrollment and placement, groups, content releases, assessments, reporting and learning access define their detailed year relationships in their own references. The student-facing organization called a “course” needs its own definition in the learning review.
